# Task 3 economic and product notes

At 200 alerts/day and 30 days/month, this implementation performs **6,000 image verifications/month**. CLIP runs locally without a hosted inference API, so recurring inference **API charges are $0**. That is not zero total cost: hardware, memory, electricity, operations and storage remain unpriced. The measured 18.79 ms warm CPU average implies about 113 seconds/month of serial image-scoring wall time at that volume on this machine, excluding model loading, text encoding, downloads, queueing and other services. This estimate is not a hardware capacity guarantee or a cloud-provider quote. The approximately 338 MiB weight download is a first-use dependency. Snapshots stay local during inference, but retention, access control and any future hosted upload still need explicit product policy.

“Flag as incorrect” should record event ID, camera/rule, timestamp, model/prompt/threshold versions, score and a review reason with a permissioned snapshot reference. A human reviewer should confirm whether the error concerns human presence, authorization, or a wrong rule, and correct mistaken flags. Retain reviewed examples with appropriate access and deletion controls. Deduplicate nearby frames and partition by camera/day before using feedback for offline prompt, threshold or model evaluation. Flags alone sample displayed false positives; audit a sample of held alerts and collect missed-event reports to measure false negatives too. Keep a separate unchanged regression set, compare candidate recall and false alarms, and roll out a versioned change with rollback only after review. Do not automatically raise a live threshold from a few negative flags. Given the measured 73.3% test recall, this baseline should prioritize review, not silently discard held alerts. A dashboard integration is a design proposal, not implemented code.

## Engineering decisions and next week

Local pretrained CLIP fits the requested secondary-verifier experiment without new training or paid APIs. A single model, explicit prompts, a calibration-only threshold and ordinary CSV/JSON artifacts keep it inspectable. CPU-only execution is intentional for reproducibility; latency was not measured on a deployed gateway. Full-frame padding preserves image edges but reduces small-target detail. Tiled inference, a detector-provided crop or a model adapted to thermal imagery are reasonable experiments, not established fixes. Ground-truth boxes must never determine inference crops in an evaluation.

With one more week: first obtain original/representative fixed-camera snapshots and authorization context; review labels and split by camera/day before modeling. Add representative animals, rain, shadows, insects and authorized-person negatives. Measure a frozen baseline, then compare one small-target approach on development data only, calibrate on independent recordings, and test once on a new holdout. Audit every missed positive, report per-scenario counts and uncertainty, and keep review-only routing until the required recall is supported. More training alone is not a substitute for relevant labels and an honest evaluation.
